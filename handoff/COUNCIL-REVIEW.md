# Council review · TLL Site Prototype 2.0 · 5 October 2026

The question: is 2.0 ready to hand to Claude Code as an award-grade build, measured against immersive storytelling, smooth motion, flawless UX, playful interaction and accessibility?

**The Contrarian**
An awards checklist is the wrong scoreboard. Awwwards rewards spectacle. TLL needs a stranger to send in a story and a dog owner to pay €9 for a PDF. The prototype now has 40+ screens, a passport, stickers, The Pack, a three-way directory and a shareables system, all resting on one editor's "reviewed within 48 hours" promise. Every new feature makes that promise harder to keep.

**The First Principles Thinker**
Everything depends on one thing: does a first-time visitor on a phone read one story and come back? So home to story to "send yours" has to be flawless on a slow connection. That makes J01 to J08 (real buttons, focus, mobile nav, image weight) worth more than any parallax. The hero photo currently ships full size.

**The Expansionist**
In three years TLL is two things: a directory of people who know places, and a passport people want to share. The paper system of tickets, tags, stamps and stickers can carry the Paw Passport print edition, the press kit and merch without a redesign. Being ahead of the content is the right way round. The roster grows into the frame.

**The Outsider**
A first-time visitor meets a boarding pass, then luggage tags, a passport, stamps, stickers and postcards. That's six metaphors in one scroll. And the Travelers page has an 87-country founder next to rows of OPEN SLOT cards, which reads like a party nobody came to yet. Also, why is one person doing the editing, the Webflow build and the brand?

**The Executor**
Ship v9 today. Build J01 to J07 on the nav, home and one story first, then the B parity rows. Freeze new features until STATUS.md shows 50 Done rows with evidence. Done means verify-live is green on /, /stories, one story, /travelers and /the-paw-passport.

**Synthesis**
Four of the five voices say the same thing in different words: the design is finished enough, and what's left is execution and focus. The Expansionist's long view argues for keeping the paper system. The Outsider's six-metaphor point is the one to act on now: v9 gives each paper object one job (DESIGN-SPEC-v9.md §1). Contrarian and Outsider both point at the empty roster. The honest fix is to show fewer open slots, not more features. Listen to the Executor this week, the Expansionist next quarter.

**Decision (Nancy, 5 October 2026):** feature freeze approved, plus one open slot per grid until the roster has three real people. After the review, the boarding pass was replaced by the mode-neutral Departures header, which also answers the Outsider's point about too many props.
