That Layover Life

Prototype · Nancy's brief of 11 Nov

A postmark for the stories you actually finished

Two small moves for the layout the site already has, written so they lift straight into a site layer. Point at the coordinates on the home hero and a View map badge tags along with your pointer. Save a story, or read one to the last line, and the magazine's ink postmark lands on the corner of its card. Both of them move only transform and opacity, so the phone never has to redo the page to show you a stamp.

01 · The coordinates

The numbers on the hero now go somewhere

On the live home page, 12.17° N 68.99° W is a caption you can admire and not much else. Here it is a link to the map. The markup below is the live hero's own paragraph, untouched; the layer turns it into the link.

With a mouse

The system pointer stays put. A small View map badge appears just under the numbers while the pointer is over them and slides along with it, moved with transform on requestAnimationFrame and nothing else. The compass option swaps the pointer for a small compass over the coordinates only, with the ordinary hand as the fallback. I would keep the badge: it says where the link goes, and the compass only says that north exists.

With a finger or a keyboard

It is a plain link, and the words View map sit under the coordinates on touch screens and appear on keyboard focus, so nobody has to guess what tapping the numbers does. Screen readers hear "Hero photo taken at 12.17° N 68.99° W Curaçao View map".

02 · The stamp

Save a story and it gets stamped

The postmark is the one on the magazine cover, inked in rose and pressed onto a cream disc so it reads on any photo. The place comes from the story's own place line and the date is the day you saved it. Press Saved again and the stamp comes off. TLL does not approve anything, so the stamp says SAVED and leaves the officialdom to the airport.

03 · Read to the end

Reach the last line and the story notices

Sample · The story text, as published, goes here

End of the story

Read it on the site →

On a story page the layer drops an invisible marker after the last paragraph of .tll-story-body. When it scrolls into view the postmark lands on the share row under the story, once per story, with READ TO THE END on its lower arc. In this box the box itself is the page: scroll it to the bottom, or tab into it and press End.

Come back to a story you finished and the stamp is already there, sitting still. It only lands the first time.

04 · Performance

What the browser does while the stamp lands

Measured on 12 Nov 2026 in headless Chromium at 390 and 1440 pixels wide, with a performance trace around each landing and a requestAnimationFrame loop running the whole time, which wakes the main thread every frame and is the worst case. Putting the stamp on the card costs one layout and four small paints, all in the frame where it appears. After that the compositor carries it, and for the rest of the 280 ms the trace shows no layout and no paint at either width. When the stamp settles, the layer takes will-change off again, and that one frame, about 300 ms after the press, costs a layout and two small paints with nothing left moving.

A headless browser on a shared machine drops the odd frame (one of our runs had a single 50 ms gap), so the honest test is the button on this page, on a real phone.

Keyframes touch only transform and opacity, so the ink filter and the multiply blend are drawn once, when the stamp appears, and never again while it moves. will-change is switched on for the 280 ms and off when the stamp settles. The badge never animates position with anything but transform, and its loop stops as soon as it catches up with the pointer.
