Nobody tells you this, but the running is not the hard part of the Antarctica Marathon. The hard part is showing up at the start line at all. We needed four attempts, four years, a global pandemic, a military quarantine, and one full marathon run inside my own apartment in Copenhagen. By the time my shoes hit that gravel, Antarctica owed me money.

What the Antarctica Marathon actually is

It is a true 42.2km marathon on King George Island in the South Shetlands, run on rough gravel and dirt roads connecting four research bases: Bellingshausen (Russia), Artigas (Uruguay), Frei (Chile), and Great Wall (China). Marathon Tours & Travel runs it in late February or early March. Because the island is small and the ice is not exactly flexible, it is always a looped course. Ours had six loops, though the number varies by year and weather.

There is also a half marathon (4.5-hour limit) and a 10K (2 hours). The full marathon has a 6-hour cutoff, and most runners take more than 5 hours to finish, which should tell you how tough the course really is.
