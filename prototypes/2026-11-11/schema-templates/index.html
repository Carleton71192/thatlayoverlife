Schema templates · November 12, 2026

Structured data, minus the wishful thinking.

One JSON-LD template for each kind of page in the brief, checked against what thatlayover.life already emits and filled with a real page to prove it parses. Google's current rules decided what went in, which is also why one template arrives with bad news attached.

01 · The short version

What each page should say about itself

Stories

Article, extended

Stories already emit a clean Article. The template keeps it and adds the reading time as timeRequired, the founder badge as a role inside TLL, the editor, and the sponsor when there is one.

Google's Article docs have no required properties. Author, dates, headline and image help it read the page correctly. None of the additions buys a special snippet.

Layover guides

Article about a City and an Airport

TravelAction describes the act of flying somewhere, not a page about it, and Google does nothing with it. The guides stay Articles and finally get an author and a typed City and Airport.

Today they carry the SEO title as the headline and no author at all.

Expat paperwork Q&A

FAQPage, for meaning only

Google stopped showing FAQ rich results for every site on May 7, 2026.

From August 2023 until then, only well-known government and health sites qualified, so TLL never would have. The markup is still valid and still describes the page. Nobody should wait for the dropdowns.

02 · The templates

Three templates and the notes JSON-LD cannot hold

Each template parses as JSON with {{PLACEHOLDER}} tokens. JSON-LD cannot hold comments, so the annotated view and the field map say where every value comes from in the Webflow CMS.

04 · The layover question

      
Four candidates for six hours in Copenhagen

      
The brief offered TravelAction or a custom Guide. Here is how each candidate fits the Layovers collection and what Google does with it.

Recommendation

      
Keep Article, which the live guides already use, and change three things: the headline becomes the guide's name instead of the SEO title with the site name stuck on, the author comes from the Layovers author reference, and about becomes a typed City plus an Airport with its IATA code. That is the most a search engine can learn from these pages without promising a feature that does not exist.

      
TouristTrip is the schema.org type that would describe the itinerary itself. It earns nothing in Google either, and the CMS has no list of stops to fill it from, so it waits until there is one.

    

  






  

    

      
05 · What Google does with it

      
The honest version of rich snippet findability

      
The brief asked for markup that maximizes rich snippets. For these three page types, Google offers fewer snippets than it used to. Here is what each piece earns.

    

    

      
Article
It helps Google read the byline
Google's Article documentation lists no required properties and recommends author (with a url), datePublished, dateModified, headline and image. It uses them to show the right title, date and byline. There is no Article-only snippet to chase.

      
FAQPage
Gone for every site since May 7, 2026
As quoted from Google's own notice by several search news sites: "As of May 7, 2026, FAQ rich results are no longer appearing in Google Search." The Rich Results Test and the Search Console report for FAQ were scheduled to go in June 2026. Test FAQ markup with the Schema Markup Validator instead.

      
Founder badge
Accurate markup with no payoff in search
No schema.org property turns a badge into a rich result. The template carries it as author.memberOf, an OrganizationRole inside TLL with the badge as roleName. Nancy's own Person node on the home page already says jobTitle "Founder and editor", which is the right place for a job.

      
Read time
timeRequired, for whoever reads it
Reading time maps to timeRequired as an ISO 8601 duration, so 9 minutes is PT9M. It is valid schema.org. Google shows no read time from it.

    

    
The build machine could not open developers.google.com, so the FAQ dates come from search results quoting Google's notice. Open Google's FAQ page and Article page once before anyone quotes them.
