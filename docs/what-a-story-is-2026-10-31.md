# What a travel story is on TLL: copy for Nancy to approve (31 Oct 2026)

Why: a generic wellness article about Thailand got published because nothing on the site says what a TLL story is. The submit page gives tips on fields, not on the thing itself. These drafts put one plain definition in the four places people meet it. Nothing is live; the /submit block is staged behind `?preview=ux`.

## The one-line test

**Could only you have written it?** If a travel desk could have written it from a press kit, it is not a TLL story.

## The definition (the same words everywhere)

A TLL story is one trip, told by the person who took it. You were there. It has a place, a time, your photos and the bit that went sideways. Not a guide, not a listicle, not a roundup of a country, not a product review. Guides and lists are fine things; they live elsewhere.

## Where it goes

### 1. /submit, above the form (staged, the block people read before they write)

**What a story looks like here**

You went. Something happened. You tell it.

- **One trip, first person.** A weekend, a layover, a year abroad. Not "Thailand" but "the night bus to Chiang Mai."
- **You were there.** Your photos, your dates, your mistakes. No stock photos, no press kits, no "top ten".
- **A place and a time.** City or country, month and year. After the trip, never during.
- **The bit that went sideways.** The missed ferry, the wrong hostel, the kindness you did not expect. That is the story.
- **In your words.** We fix typos. AI writes nothing here, and anything AI made is labeled.

Not sure? Ask: could only you have written it? Then it belongs here.

What we send back: guides, roundups, "best of" lists, hotel or product reviews, anything written to sell something. We will tell you why, kindly, and point you to where it fits.

### 2. The story end block (the "Pitch a layover story" ask), one line under the button

"One trip, told by the person who took it. Could only you have written it? Then send it."

### 3. /stories empty search state (replacing "Nothing filed for … yet")

"Nothing filed for that yet. Every story here is one trip, told by someone who went. Yours could be the first."

### 4. Editorial charter, a new rule (Nancy edits the page; draft only)

**What we publish.** A story is one trip, told first person by the person who took it, with their own photos and a place and time. We do not publish guides, roundups, lists, reviews or anything that could have been written without going. Practical pages (visa trackers, pet rules, train rules) are not stories; they link their official sources and say when we last checked.

### 5. FAQ entry

**What counts as a story?** One trip, first person, your photos, a place and a time, and the part that went wrong. If a travel desk could have written it from a press kit, it is not one. Guides, lists and reviews go elsewhere on the site.

## Also suggested

- **The "What" field on /submit** gets a placeholder: "The night bus to Chiang Mai, not Thailand."
- **A submit-time check, gentle:** if the headline contains "best", "top", "guide", "destination", "luxury" or "ultimate", show one line under it: "Sounds like a guide. Stories here are one trip, told by the person who took it. Keep going if that is what this is." No blocking.
- **Series stories** (The Free Evening, The First Friend, Cats Who Travel) already follow this: they ask for a city, a date and the writer's own fields.
