## Method
- Harness: the A3 author's h3.cjs (itself from the regression check's h.cjs) copied to scratchpad nov14/a3adv/bin/h4.cjs with the same caches and routing, plus new faults. www HTML from the existing plain-GET copies, served at the www address. W is the 16.64.0 core with its integrity kept; A2 (16.65.0, pin 0daa78b4, the regression check's core-new.js) and A3 (the local build above) are served in place of the core tag with integrity removed in the routed HTML only. Fresh browser context per load, the three cores in turn, order reversed every other round.
- Chromium only (headless, the build in the environment; WebKit and Firefox are not installed). Unthrottled, and slow 4G as in the earlier checks (150 ms per request, one 1.6 Mbit/s pipe, CPU 4x). CLS is the sum of every layout-shift entry.
- Synthetic stories: local copies of the saved Croatia or Balkans HTML with text edits only (gen.py in the scratchpad). Croatia has a country slug, so the template makes a crumb link; Balkans does not.
  - zz-long-place: a 70-letter place with one country (France).
  - zz-twelve: twelve countries as a comma list (4 flags and "+8 countries" with a long crumb).
  - zz-five-link: five countries. zz-one, zz-one-link: one country without and with a link.
  - zz-us-state: "New Orleans, Louisiana". zz-unknown: a place with no flag. zz-georgia: a name that needs the Intl table.
  - zz-empty-crumb: empty crumb text with a country slug (the link takes the country name).
  - zz-no-hero-img, zz-no-hero: no hero photo; no hero section at all. zz-no-receipts: no Receipts block. zz-no-byline: no byline row.
  - zz-long-title, zz-iso-date (a date with no month name), zz-no-flag-tag (no flag script tag).
- Faults: dropFlags (flag script 404), throwFlags (its body replaced by a throw), abortFlags (network error), noFlagScript (tag removed), slowFlags (2.5 s late), failTmpl (throw at the top of the template script), tmplPlaceThrow (throw inside the template's place step, after its date step), abortHero, pause-hero and pause-receipts (a parser-blocking 700 ms script placed right after the hero section, or right before the Receipts block, so the browser paints a partly parsed page), banner-fixed and banner-top (nodes added to the body while it is parsed: a fixed consent-style panel and iframe, or a 60 px block at the top), a stub consent tool (no answer, or consent at 3 s), reduced motion, JavaScript off, back and forward, resize to 390, 320 and 1440 after load, and rotation 390x844 to 844x390 and back.

## Findings, worst first
1. **A failed flag script costs 0.14 on a Balkans-like story.** When tllflagsv53 returns 404, fails on the network, throws, or is missing from the page, Balkans at 360 scores 0.139 to 0.150 on every A3 load (zz-no-flag-tag included), against 0.017 to 0.020 on www and 0.017 to 0.021 on A2. The stand-in (four boxes and "+8 countries") is painted, then taken away when no flags come, and the breadcrumb drops from 73 to 44 px under the painted head. The author listed only the 404 for this (0.138) and reported "flag script throws" as 0.014; that number holds on Southern Africa (0.012 here) but a throw is as bad as a 404 on Balkans. On Southern Africa and Croatia the flag faults are harmless (0.012 and 0.007). The shift cannot be avoided once the stand-in is painted; the only cure is a flag script that does not fail. A failure of one Webflow-hosted script is rare, so I rate this a note, not a block.
2. **A failed template script costs 0.15 to 0.17 on Southern Africa.** With a throw at the top of tll-st3-story-js-v1, Southern Africa at 390 scores 0.165 on both A3 loads (www 0.058, A2 0.074); with a throw in its place step, 0.150 (www 0.064, A2 0.081). The crumb loses the link box and the place-and-date words go back to the old ones after the first paint. On Croatia A3 is 0.061 (www 0.13, A2 0.010 to 0.013) and on Balkans 0.034 to 0.050 (www 0.018, A2 0.15). The author reported 0.096 against 0.067 for this case; on Southern Africa it is worse than that. The finished page matched www in every load. The template script is inline in the page, so it fails only if it is edited badly.
3. **Long multi-country crumbs: the stand-in is wider than the real flags.** On zz-twelve the first-frame breadcrumb is 142 px (124 px after the web font) and 106 px once the real flags are in, so A3 scores 0.062 at 360 and 0.054 at 390 against A2 0.036 and 0.035 (www 0.038 and 0.14; at 320 A3 is lowest, 0.029). The cause: the flag script's images get Webflow's site rule img {max-width:100%}, which makes them compressible, so the flag group can shrink to the width of the word "countries" (52 px here) while the images keep 22 px and overflow it. The stand-in boxes are spans with flex:none and do not shrink (115 px). Southern Africa, Balkans, zz-five-link and zz-long-place show it only slightly or not at all (A3 0.002 to 0.019). Suggested fix: build the stand-in boxes from elements that size like the real images (empty img elements with the same inline style, or spans that may shrink). Also seen on www and every core: when the group is squeezed the flags overlap the first letters of the crumb by 3 px on Southern Africa at 360 and 9 px on zz-twelve. That is a flag script layout issue, not part of A3.
4. **HTML that stalls right after the hero.** With a 700 ms parser stall between the hero section and the byline row, Balkans at 360 scores 0.089 on A3 against 0.019 on www (A2 0.155). The head is painted before PF has run, and the stand-in then pushes it down 29 px. A stall before the Receipts block does no harm (0.009), because PF runs as soon as the byline row is whole when no Receipts block has started. Southern Africa and Croatia are fine in both positions (0.006 to 0.012). The open window is about 3 KB of HTML, from the country crumb to the end of the byline row; it matters on repeat visits over slow links, when the core is cached and the HTML is still arriving. The lab's slow 4G profile delivers the HTML whole, so it never shows this. Placing the flag stand-in as soon as the crumb itself is parsed (it needs only the crumb text, and the country name for an empty crumb) would close most of it.
5. **US state crumbs are still not reserved.** zz-us-state ("New Orleans, Louisiana") at 360: A3 0.150, A2 0.138, www 0.204, over 0.1 on every load of every core. Almost all of it is one 0.128 shift about 1.4 s after DOMContentLoaded, when tll-story-fixes-1029 adds the US flag. At 390 A3 is 0.012. This is the author's known risk 5; worth a follow-up because any US-state story at phone widths will score this.
6. **Photo near the fold can get the reveal fade.** In banner-top (a 60 px block inserted above the page while it loads) on Balkans 360, the A3 finished page differed from www and A2: the photo kept the reveal script's start state (opacity 0.6, 8 px down) until scrolled. tllreveal marks a hero child that starts below 92 percent of the window at DOMContentLoaded. On A3 the head already has its final height at that moment, so the photo starts at 720 px (the line is 718); on A2 and www the breadcrumb was still 29 px shorter when the reveal script looked. This is the reveal rule working as designed on the true layout, not an A3 fault, but it is the one case where A3's finished page was not www's.

## What held up
- Every hold clears and nothing stays hidden: across all 753 loads no A3 load ended with data-tll-mg-wait-r, data-tll-mg-wait-d, the flag stand-in or the early style on the page, and no element in the story head was left hidden or at opacity 0.
- Finished page: in every unthrottled story case the A3 layout, style and text hashes equal www's, except banner-top above (on zz-no-hero-img at 360 the two www loads differed from each other; A3 matched A2 and one of them). On slow 4G, 3 of 12 A3, 4 of 12 A2 and 5 of 12 www loads ended with the same text in a different order, consistent with the known race at the foot of the story (regression check item 13).
- Page errors: none except the injected ones. Console error counts: www 1438, A2 1437, A3 1437 over the same loads (Memberstack offline, aborted requests).
- Slow flag script (2.5 s): A3 0.009 to 0.012; the stand-in holds the space until the real flags arrive.
- Hero photo aborted: A3 0.007 to 0.014.
- No hero photo, no Receipts block, no byline row, long title, odd date, Georgia (Intl table), unknown place, one country, empty crumb: A3 0.004 to 0.039, lower than or equal to A2 in every case but zz-unknown (0.011 and 0.019 against 0.006 and 0.008) and zz-one-link at 390 (0.012 against 0.005), all small.
- No hero section at all: PF does nothing (no hero), so A3 runs A2's path; all three cores vary from load to load (0.12 to 0.44, the route strip under the nav).
- Reduced motion, consent stub (no answer: no ad or analytics request in 6 s on any core; consent at 3 s: the tags load after it on all three), fixed consent panel and iframe added while parsing: A3 0.007 to 0.019, finished page clean.
- JavaScript off: identical on all three.
- Back and forward: as in the regression check, headless Chromium under request routing never restored from the back/forward cache (pageshow persisted false), so the back navigation is a reload and matches the first load. A3 adds no pageshow, pagehide or unload handler, and all its state is gone one task after DOMContentLoaded, so a page restored from that cache has nothing pending.
- Resize after load (360 to 390, 320 and 1440) and rotation (390x844 to 844x390 and back): the finished layout after each step is the same on all three cores, with the same shift totals.
- Non-story pages (/, /stories, /travelers, /layovers, a contributor profile, a 404 under /stories/): the story block returns at its path test, so the code is A2's. Numbers are within the pages' own spread: /travelers about 0.11 on every core; /stories at 1440 has a two-state shift (0.049 or 0.111) that showed up in 2 of 6 www, 1 of 6 A2 and 3 of 6 A3 loads with the same sources each time. The 404 under /stories/ runs the story style on all three cores, as before.

## Recommendation
A3 is the better core: on every saved story and width I measured it is at or below www and far below A2 where A2 regressed, and the finished page is unchanged. Before it goes to the test site, two small changes would remove most of the residue: make the stand-in boxes size like the real flag images (finding 3), and put the flag stand-in in as soon as the crumb is parsed (finding 4). Findings 1 and 2 need a script failure and are the price of reserving space before the scripts run. Finding 5 is a separate follow-up.
