## 1. Six lines for Nancy

1. **What the page is for.** The members-only upload form for The Layover Lounge: a signed-in Traveler drops up to 12 photos, says where they were taken, picks a credit, ticks three promises about rights, and sends. Signed out, the same page is the sign-in wall for that form.
2. **What is wrong today.** Sixteen controls across five numbered chapters on a page that promises no full story is needed; a dark hero band over a paper form where the prototype draws one column; visible example placeholders, one with an emoji; nine toggles in a row; three CSS blocks fighting over the same checkboxes; no feedback after choosing photos; a success box that contradicts the status line and promises a withdraw feature no account page has; duplicate DOM ids; and underneath all of it a plain GET form whose photo files most likely never arrive anywhere.
3. **What changes.** The page becomes the prototype's §38 screen: one 640px Paper column, four mono group labels, a dashed drop zone with a real file list, two story inputs, a three-way credit row, three promise cards, one full-width Rose button and a one-line footnote. City, year, trip type, the pet field and the three opt-ins stay in the form but fold under two collapsed disclosures. Every empty, error and success state is designed, with inline errors in voice. The signed-out gate gets a three-line public preview. All of it is page-level CSS, one page-level script and copy edits.
4. **What stays.** Every input, name, id and data-* attribute; the Memberstack gate and its two content blocks; the form's name, method, action, enctype and destination (Webflow forms); the three promise sentences, their explanations, the Community Guidelines link and the required flags on the promises; the back link to /gallery; noindex, follow. No site-wide block is edited.
5. **Required inputs.** Five at markup level today (country, year, three promises), with the photo demanded by the error copy but never enforced. Five after, now the right five (photo, where, three promises), enforced inline by the page script and by the browser if the script does not load.
6. **What only you can decide.** The upload target (A1, A2 or A3) before the page promises anything; year optional or required; the review and withdrawal wording; numbered or plain group labels; eyebrow color; what happens to the three opt-ins; the duplicate ids; the new page code; the gate preview. Full list in section 6.

## 2. The first screen, top to bottom

### Signed in, desktop (1280 x 800)

1. TLL Nav v1, Ink, unchanged.
2. A 640px Paper (#FDF9F3) column, 9vh top padding, no dark band.
3. Back link "← The Layover Lounge", JetBrains Mono 11px 600 teal #067A79, uppercase via CSS, to /gallery.
4. Eyebrow "The Layover Lounge", mono 11px 600, tracking 3px, uppercase via CSS, teal.
5. Title "Submit your shot." in Playfair Display 800 at clamp(34px, 4.2vw, 64px), line-height .95, Ink, with "shot" in italic Rose text #C8325B.
6. Lede "Real photos from real places. No filters, no stock, no staging. If you were there, it counts." IBM Plex Sans Condensed 15px, #6B6660, 26px above the first group.
7. Group label "01 · YOUR PHOTOS" (mono 10px teal; the number comes from CSS, see row 9) and the help line "Up to 12 at a time. JPG or WebP, 25MB each at most."
8. The drop zone: white card, 2px dashed border, 4px radius, 30px padding, centered: "↑" glyph, "DRAG PHOTOS HERE OR TAP TO CHOOSE" in mono, the originals note, and "Phone-shot is fine. We crop to fit." Nothing under it until a file is chosen.
9. Group label "02 · THE STORY", its help line, and the "Where is this?" field. "What was happening?" starts at the fold.

A first-time reader gets the purpose from the title and lede. A would-be contributor sees the one thing to do. An advertiser reads "real photos from real places" under the Lounge's name.

### Signed in, phone (390 x 844)

Same order with 48px top padding and 16px side gutters: nav, back link, eyebrow, title (two lines at 34px), lede, the photos group label and help, and the whole drop zone. The story label and the "Where is this?" field arrive on the first scroll.

### Signed out, desktop and phone

1. TLL Nav v1.
2. The same 640px column and type scale as the signed-in page.
3. Eyebrow "The Layover Lounge" (mono 11px teal).
4. Headline "Sign in to drop your photos." with "drop your photos" in Rose text, at the title scale.
5. Lede "A photo, not an essay. Three short steps, and a human on the other end."
6. Three preview rows, each a mono 10px teal word beside a 15px sentence: Drop, Where, Send (copy in section 4).
7. One 15px line for readers and advertisers: "Every photo in the Lounge was taken by a member and sent with their permission."
8. Buttons "Log in" (Rose #D63859, white text) and "Join the Travelers" (Ink outline), 48px, side by side on desktop, stacked under 480px.
9. A 12px #6B6660 line: "Free. Travelers are 16 and older."

A first-time reader learns what the Lounge is, a would-be contributor sees the whole ask before signing in, an advertiser reads that the photos are member-shot and permissioned.
