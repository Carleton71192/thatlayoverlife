# Publisher listing copy

Written against TLL BrandKit v2 (4 August 2026). Locked tagline and positioning
statement used verbatim where they fit. US English, no em dashes.

A note on register. These are review forms read by a platform reviewer deciding
whether to carry you, so this copy runs closer to the positioning statement than
to the unhinged end of the voice. It is on brand, not off leash. The hashtag
energy and the signed sign-offs belong in the posts, which is what the reviewer
clicks through to anyway.

---

## Publication name

```
That Layover Life
```

Use this exactly, every platform, no suffix. Not "That Layover Life Travel Blog"
and not "That Layover Life Magazine".

## Tagline (locked)

```
The world is your layover.
```

## Descriptions by length

**Under 60 characters** (Apple News tagline, Flipboard magazine subtitle)

```
Character-led travel stories from named travelers.
```

**Under 160 characters** (Google News Publisher Center, meta description)

```
Honest, vivid travel stories from named travelers about specific places. For people with thirty countries on the board who still feel like they have not seen anything.
```

**Under 300 characters** (MSN Partner Hub, Apple News description)

```
That Layover Life publishes honest, character-led travel writing from named travelers about specific places. Not listicles, not algorithm-shaped sludge. Stories with a person in them, a meal in them, and usually a stray animal. The world is your layover.
```

**Long form** (Flipboard profile, About pages, anywhere with room)

```
That Layover Life is a travel platform for people who have already been everywhere and still feel like they have not seen anything yet.

We publish honest, vivid, character-led travel stories from named travelers about specific places. Every piece has a person in it, a specific meal, and a sense of what the air was like. Travel content has become algorithm-shaped sludge that reads as though nobody actually went. We are the correction.

We take the layover seriously as a unit of travel. Six hours in a city you did not choose teaches you more than seven days in one you researched to death.

The world is your layover.
```

## Category and topic selection

Pick these where the form offers them:

- Primary: **Travel**
- Secondary: **Lifestyle**, then **Food and Drink** if a third is allowed
- Not "News", and not "Aviation". News invites a standard TLL cannot meet as a
  single-author publication, and aviation misdescribes the writing.

## Language and region

- Language: **English (United States)**
- Country of publication: wherever you are legally publishing from. Be consistent
  with what Search Console and your other listings say.

## Fields worth answering carefully

**"Who writes your content?"**

```
Named individual travelers, with bylines. Founder and lead writer Nancy Carleton, plus contributed stories from travelers who are credited by name.
```

**"How often do you publish?"**

Answer honestly with your real cadence. Overstating this is the fastest way to
fail a review, because reviewers check the archive against the claim.

**"Do you use AI-generated content?"**

```
No. Every story is written by a named human traveler about a place they actually went.
```

This is true and it is worth saying plainly. Platforms are actively filtering for
it in 2026, and it happens to be TLL's entire position.

## Before you submit anywhere

1. The RSS feed validates and shows recent posts.
2. `NewsArticle` schema passes the Rich Results Test on a live post.
3. There is a real About page with a named human on it.
4. There is a contact method that is not a form, ideally an email address.
5. Privacy policy exists. Google News and Apple News both check.

Items 3, 4 and 5 fail more submissions than content quality does.
